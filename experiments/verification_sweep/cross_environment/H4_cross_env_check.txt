H4 cross-environment certification check -- the ansatz control
==============================================================
Date        2026-09-04

Two entries, same molecule, same basis, same mapping, same drifted environment. The only
difference is the ansatz and the optimiser it is handed to. This is the cleanest control
available in the suite for isolating what makes an entry environment-fragile.

    H4_ccpvdz_JW_ADAPT_v4_tapered__sha256_39f9134a722ad612.json    ADAPT-VQE (COBYLA inner)
    H4_ccpvdz_JW_HEA_v4_tapered__sha256_2cb5ee33e450acbd.json      HEA, plain COBYLA

Why         After H10 survived the thinnest margin in the suite, the working rule was
            "gradient-free optimisers amplify, gradient-based ones do not", and the
            highest-risk remaining entry was H4/ADAPT: a 0.58% margin AND a COBYLA inner
            optimiser. That rule predicted H4/ADAPT would be the next failure.
            It was wrong.

Environment tested (drifted against the pinned reference):
    python      3.11.14   (pinned 3.11.15)
    pyscf       2.5.0     (pinned 2.6.2)
    scipy       1.17.0    (pinned 1.13.1)
    pennylane   0.44.1    (pinned 0.45.0)
    numpy       1.26.4    (pinned 2.2.6)     <- major version difference

Command
    python scripts/verify_entry.py <entry> --mode certification \
        --allow-dirty --allow-env-drift

Results
    entry                     stored gap    regen gap    energy moved   verdict   elapsed
    H4 ADAPT (COBYLA inner)   9.942e-03     9.942e-03    3.428e-08      PASS       11 s
    H4 HEA   (plain COBYLA)   9.283e-03     8.405e-03    8.774e-04      PASS       61 s

Reading

1.  Gradient-free is not sufficient for fragility. H4/ADAPT was flagged AT-RISK by the
    optimiser-family rule and moved 3.428e-08 Ha -- 1692x LESS than its own margin, and
    the smallest movement measured anywhere in the suite.

2.  The ansatz is the variable, not the optimiser alone. Holding molecule, basis, mapping
    and environment fixed, HEA moved 25595x more than ADAPT under the same optimiser
    family. ADAPT selects its operators by analytic gradient, so the ansatz structure is
    gradient-determined and COBYLA only polishes a small, incrementally grown and
    well-conditioned parameter set. HEA hands COBYLA a full unstructured parameter vector
    over a landscape with many near-degenerate minima, which is where a flipped comparison
    can select a different basin.

3.  Every measured entry, ranked by movement against its own margin:

        entry                          margin Ha    moved Ha   moved/margin
        H4   ADAPT / COBYLA inner      5.800e-05   3.428e-08         0.001x
        H10  ADAPT / L-BFGS-B inner    2.300e-05   1.005e-06         0.04x
        H4   HEA   / COBYLA            7.170e-04   8.774e-04         1.22x
        C4H4 HEA   / COBYLA (PAR)      3.891e-03   1.443e-02         3.71x

    The two ADAPT entries sit three orders of magnitude below their margins. Both HEA
    entries moved MORE than their own margin. The separation is clean with no overlap:
    ADAPT <= 1.0e-06 Ha, HEA >= 8.8e-04 Ha.

4.  H4/HEA passed on the sign, not on robustness. It moved 1.22x its own margin and
    survived only because the energy moved TOWARD the reference, shrinking the gap from
    9.283 to 8.405 mHa. Had the movement gone the other way it would have failed. It is
    recorded as marginal rather than robust: a pass whose direction was luck is not
    evidence of stability.

    This is the mirror image of the trap already recorded in LEADERBOARD_RULES_V2.md --
    that gap + |dE| over-predicts failure because movement can be favourable. The same
    fact means a pass does not prove a small movement.

Conclusion
    Amplification risk is the conjunction of a gradient-free optimiser AND an unstructured
    ansatz, not the optimiser alone. The risk flag in tools/certification_margin.py was
    updated accordingly; it previously named 6 at-risk entries, of which 4 were ADAPT
    entries that this measurement indicates are not the concern.

Caveat
    n = 5 measured entries. The ADAPT/HEA separation is three orders of magnitude with no
    overlap, but it rests on two ADAPT measurements. It is stated as what has been
    measured, not as a proven law.
